For Darryl · Valletta

An alternative, on the side.

This is not "quit The Pub and gamble." It's a part-time path into security work: finding the weak points in systems before the wrong people do, and getting paid to say what you found. You can start this week, with a device already on its way to you, and never miss a shift.

Here's why I think it suits you in particular, what the first six months actually look like, the gear, and where the real jobs are in Malta.

Why you, specifically

01

You read people for a living

Fifteen years clocking who's trouble, who's lying, who's about to leave without paying. Security is reading a system the same way. That instinct is the job.

02

Rugby taught you phases

You already think in attack and defence, in finding the gap in a line before it closes. Red team and blue team is that, with keyboards.

03

You can grind

A double shift on your feet is stamina most people don't have. This work rewards patience more than genius. You've got the patience.

The one rule

Non-negotiable

Only test what's yours, or what someone has invited you to test in writing.

Everything else is a crime, however good your intentions. Malta learned this the hard way: in 2022 some students found a flaw in a local app, reported it to be helpful, and got arrested. The law has since moved to protect researchers who follow a company's published rules. Follow the rules and you're a professional. Skip them and you're the cautionary tale. The platforms below give you written permission from day one, so you never have to guess.

The first six months

A few hours a week, most of it free, all of it while you keep working. Nobody checks a certificate at the door of this field. They check whether you can do it.

Weeks 1–2 · does this even grab me?

Poke at it, cost nothing

Make a free account on TryHackMe and do the intro rooms on your phone between shifts. Flash Bruce onto the Cardputer Matt's sending you and feel radio, NFC and signals as real things you can touch, not theory.

You finish the first room and genuinely want the next one.
Weeks 3–8 · foundations

How systems actually talk

Networking basics, a bit of Linux, how a request becomes a response. This is the boring-but-everything layer. TryHackMe's beginner path walks you through it. Thirty minutes a day beats a three-hour binge once a month.

You solve a machine start to finish without a walkthrough.
Weeks 9–16 · pick a lane

Defend, or probe

Choose blue team (protect and watch) or red team (test and break, with permission). Go deep on one. Aim at a recognised entry cert like CompTIA Security+ if you want a badge employers recognise, or straight at practice if you'd rather prove it by doing.

A cert booked, or your first written-up challenge online.
Weeks 17–24 · prove it in the open

Real targets, real permission

Practice on Hack The Box, then try a live program on Intigriti (European, Malta-friendly) where companies pay you to find flaws inside a set scope. Write up everything you do. Those writeups are your CV in this field.

Your first valid finding, or your first interview. And the Flipper One as your reward.

The two lanes, up close

Blue team · defence

You hold the line

Watch the system, spot the intrusion, shut it down. Steady, in-demand, the bulk of the actual jobs.

  • Monitoring and responding to threats
  • Hardening systems so they don't fall over
  • The calm under pressure of a last-orders rush, applied to an incident
Red team · offence

You find the gap first

Probe a system you're hired to probe, find the way in before a criminal does, report it. Flashier, and where the bug-bounty money lives.

  • Testing apps and networks on request
  • Thinking like the opposition, legally
  • Getting paid per flaw you find and prove

It's a contact sport. Offence probes, defence holds, and you study the other side's phases before they run them.

Where the work is, in Malta

iGaming

The island's biggest employer, and every operator is regulated to the teeth on security and fraud. Steady demand for people who can defend systems and pass audits.

Fintech & blockchain

Malta leaned hard into financial tech. MFSA-regulated firms need security people, and the pay reflects it.

Bug bounty, from anywhere

You don't need a Maltese employer at all. Programs on Intigriti and others pay per finding, from your couch, in your own hours.

The legal tailwind

Malta is actively building legal cover for researchers who follow the rules. The field is opening here, not closing.
