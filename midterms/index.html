In short

Bayesian, here
Start with a belief and update it as votes arrive. The belief comes from final polls and race ratings. Early counts move it only a little, because the first votes counted are rarely typical.
Alpha · cautious
The Bayesian engine. It assumes the uncounted vote can differ from the counted vote by about 20 points. Replayed on 2024, its worst call left Baldwin at 9% with 94% counted in Wisconsin. She won.
Beta · aggressive
Compares each county with how it voted in 2024, then shifts every uncounted county by the same amount. It spots a wave early and gets it wrong when the shift is uneven.
Ensemble · the needle
Blends the two, race by race. Before any votes it is pure Alpha; Beta earns up to 60% of the say by the time half a state is counted. When they disagree, it calls the race a toss-up instead of averaging.
Gamma · the scoreboard
Only races the AP has called. It moves in steps and is the line the other engines are racing to beat.
TV desks
Gemini reads race-call graphics off live network broadcasts. A call counts only when two frames, or two networks, agree.
Chance of control
4,000 simulated nights, each with one polling miss shared by every state. The percentage is the share of nights the Democrats reach 51 Senate or 218 House seats.

How the forecast works

From races to control

Chances of control come from 4,000 simulated nights per engine. Polling misses run the same way across states, as they did in 2016, 2020 and 2024, so each night draws one national error z shared by every race and one local error per race [5]. The headline is a probability with a range, not a call [6].

error_i = σ_i · (ρ·z + √(1 − ρ²)·ε_i)     ρ = 0.5, so two races' errors correlate at ρ² = 0.25
P(control) = share of 4,000 nights with Dems at 51 Senate or 218 House seats
